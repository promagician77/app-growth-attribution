Why approval day is not measurement day

Your app is already live, so on release day three things are true at once.

  • Users who update are not installs. They carry no campaign attribution, because there was no ad click. Counting them in a cost-per-install denominator makes every channel look better than it is.
  • Users who install fresh from that day are fully measurable.
  • Users who never update are invisible to the new events entirely.

So the readable paid cohort starts at zero on release day and grows. A build approved on 1 December gives you a thin December and a good January, which is the wrong way round for a December to January sale.

Why purchases are dispatched from your server

A device-fired purchase event is trivially spoofable, and a paid campaign optimising toward a spoofable event will reliably go and find the people who spoof it. Purchases are verified against Apple and Google first, written to your own ledger, then dispatched.

That ledger, not a dashboard, is the number you reconcile against when the sale ends.

The refund path, which is the step everyone skips

Apple sends a CONSUMPTION_REQUEST when a refund is opened and a REFUND or REFUND_DECLINED with the outcome, through App Store Server Notifications V2. Google Play has an equivalent through Real-time Developer Notifications. Without that endpoint, your December return on ad spend reports revenue that went back to the customer in January.

Google accepts a refund event. Meta has no refund event at all, so that correction is applied in the reporting layer instead.

What we do about it

  • Encode a predictor, not the outcome. The conversion value set inside the first 48 hours carries the signals that correlate with converting: onboarding finished, first workout logged, paywall reached, trial started. The campaign optimises toward a leading indicator that fits inside the window. On iOS this is the whole game.
  • Consider a shorter trial for paid traffic only. A 3 day trial converts inside window 1. That is a commercial decision and it moves conversion rate as well as measurement, so it stays yours. Worth modelling in November instead of discovering in January. Organic users keep the longer trial.
  • Keep a server-side truth table. Your own database knows exactly which install converted and when. Attribution is lossy on iOS. Blended reporting against a holdout answers the question you actually have, which is whether the spend is working.

Android is not the same

Google Play's Install Referrer returns the full referrer URL with click and install-begin timestamps, and it stays available for 90 days. Android attribution is comparatively complete. A blended iOS and Android number will hide a problem that exists on only one of them, so the reports separate the two platforms from day one.

One more platform limit worth knowing in advance

Meta caps how many iOS campaigns a single app can run at once. The plan uses four campaigns deliberately, not fifteen, and low-volume campaigns also lose conversion detail to Apple's anonymity threshold. Splitting a sale budget across many small campaigns can return less usable data than the same spend concentrated.

Push is a build item

Two of the requirements for promotional push are screens, not settings. Both ship in the November build.

App Store Review Guideline 4.5.4, quoted from Apple's published guidelines

Push Notifications must not be required for the app to function, and should not be used to send sensitive personal or confidential information. Push Notifications should not be used for promotions or direct marketing purposes unless customers have explicitly opted in to receive them via consent language displayed in your app's UI, and you provide a method in your app for a user to opt out from receiving such messages.

That sentence contains two separate build requirements. The iOS system permission prompt satisfies neither of them: it asks whether notifications may be delivered and says nothing about promotions.

LayerWho asksGovernsReversible in app
iOS system authorizationApple's promptWhether any notification is deliveredNo. The user has to go to Settings
Your marketing consentYour own screenWhether promotional sends are allowedYes, through your settings toggle

The prompt is a one-shot resource

On iOS the system prompt can be shown once. If the user declines, you cannot show it again from inside the app. Prompting on first launch, before anyone has a reason to want notifications, spends your single attempt at the moment it is worth least.

What ships instead is a short priming screen with a clear reason, triggered after a first completed workout. The system prompt is shown only to users who say yes on the priming screen. Users who say no keep their system prompt unspent and can be asked again later.

Delivery plumbing

  • APNs auth key, a .p8, uploaded once. Preferred over certificates, which expire annually and will choose a weekend to do it.
  • FCM's legacy server-key API is retired. Sends go through the HTTP v1 endpoint with a service account and hourly OAuth tokens, so the sending credential is no longer a string anyone can paste into a tool.
  • Device tokens rotate. A send list that is never refreshed quietly decays, so the runbook covers the refresh job and what a rising invalid-token rate means.

Three channels, what each one needs

Split by where the work happens, because that decides what can still be changed after the binary is locked.

Google

Events through Firebase. Conversions imported into Google Ads from the linked property, so no second SDK is added for the ads side.

  • Linking is reversible, so it is done in week one
  • Imported conversions lag by about a day on first appearance
  • App campaigns need seven to ten stable days before their numbers mean anything

Meta

Meta SDK in the binary. Value and currency must be populated or a Purchase is dropped on arrival.

  • Aggregated Event Measurement ranking is set once, correctly
  • Reordering that list resets a 72 hour window every time
  • Domain and app verification is the slowest step, so it starts first
  • iOS campaigns per app are capped, so the plan uses four

Apple Search Ads

Reports downloads in its own dashboard with no SDK at all. Attributing an in-app purchase back to a campaign is what needs code.

  • AdServices token read on first open
  • Token expires 24 hours after install
  • Resolved server side with retries, never on device
  • A 404 on the first attempt is normal, not a bug

Why the token is resolved on your server

The attribution token has a 24 hour lifetime. Resolving it on the device means a user who opens the app once and then goes offline for a day becomes permanently unattributable. Posting the token to your server on first open and resolving it there, with retries, keeps that user.

Accounts

Every account is created under your company. I am added as a user and can be removed in one click without anything breaking. There is no account in this project that lives under my name. This is worth checking with anyone you hire, because it is the most common way a handoff turns out not to be a handoff.

January is a different job

For an exercise app, the first two weeks of January are the largest revenue event of the year. Install attribution cannot see any of it.

Those users are not new installs. They installed months ago, stopped opening the app, and came back because it is January. There is no ad click to attribute and no install to count. Every acquisition dashboard you own will report that revenue as organic, or as belonging to whichever campaign happens to be running.

How it gets measured instead

  • Define the lapsed segment from session_start. No session in 30 days is a reasonable starting line, and it is why that event is in the spec even though no ad platform receives it.
  • Hold out 10% at random and send them nothing at all.
  • Compare the two groups on return rate and on revenue. That difference is the only honest number for what push is worth to you.
  • Report it separately from acquisition, always. Blending re-engagement revenue into a campaign report is the fastest way to conclude that a channel works when it does not.

Re-engagement ads on iOS are click-through only, so expect thin view-through data there and do not read its absence as the campaign failing.

WindowWhat you are buyingMeasured by
Late NovemberInstalls before the sale, at pre-holiday pricesInstall attribution, normally
December saleConversion of a warm baseServer ledger, refund adjusted
JanuaryReturn of users you already hadHoldout, not attribution

Three windows, three different questions, three different ways of answering them. A single blended return-on-ad-spend number across all three is not a measurement, and it is the number most dashboards will offer you by default.

Self-managing after handoff

The test the runbook has to pass: someone on your team who was in none of our calls can run the December sale from the document alone.

Safe to change

  • Daily budgets within the stated bands
  • Pausing a campaign that is clearly losing money
  • Ad creative and copy
  • Push copy, timing and segments
  • Adding a keyword to Search Ads

Ask first

  • Changing the optimisation event on a live campaign
  • Reordering the Meta event priority list
  • Anything that renames an event or parameter
  • Adding a new ad platform SDK

Never without a developer

  • Editing the event dispatch layer
  • Changing how a purchase is verified

The weekly routine, about 30 minutes

01
Open the one report. Not the three dashboards.
02
Read Android and iOS separately. They are not comparable.
03
Compare spend against refund-adjusted revenue, never gross.
04
Change at most one thing per campaign per week.

Step four is the one that gets broken. Google App campaigns and Meta both need roughly a week of stable conditions before their output means anything. Daily budget edits during a two week sale produce a campaign that never leaves the learning phase and a report nobody can read.

Three things to say out loud before January

  • The platforms will disagree with each other and with your bank. Each one claims conversions it believes it caused, and some of those overlap. The reconciliation sheet shows the size of gap that is normal.
  • iOS data arrives late and coarse. A campaign that looks dead on day two can read perfectly well on day eight.
  • Your server ledger is the truth. Ad platforms are for steering. The ledger is for counting.
